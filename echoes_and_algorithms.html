01 / Welcome

About This Portfolio

A journey through the changing world of media and information.

Welcome to Echoes & Algorithms: Navigating the Media Spectrum, an electronic portfolio designed as a small interactive museum. It explores how people create, distribute, access, interpret, and share information through different forms of media.

From printed newspapers and radio broadcasts to social networking sites, websites, and interactive technologies, media continues to evolve. Each form offers opportunities to learn and communicate, but each also requires us to think carefully about the messages we encounter.

This portfolio aims to develop media awareness, critical thinking, responsible participation, and the habit of checking information before accepting or sharing it.

🎯

Purpose

To present the media spectrum in an organized and engaging way while encouraging responsible and informed media use.

🔎

Learning objectives

Identify forms of media, understand their roles, evaluate information sources, and recognize the responsibilities of media users.

🌱

Key message

Being connected is not the same as being informed. We must question, verify, and think critically about the content we consume.

02 / The media spectrum

Understanding Media

What media is, how it works, and why it matters.

Media refers to the channels, tools, and platforms used to communicate information, ideas, stories, and messages to an audience. It can inform people about current events, support education, entertain, preserve culture, and encourage public discussion.

Media messages are created by people or organizations for particular purposes and audiences. Understanding who created a message, why it was created, and how it is presented helps us interpret it more carefully.

📢

To inform

Media shares news, announcements, research, and explanations that help people understand events and issues.

🎓

To educate

Learning materials, documentaries, tutorials, and educational websites help people develop knowledge and skills.

🎭

To entertain

Films, music, games, shows, and online videos provide enjoyment and creative experiences.

🗣️

To persuade

Advertisements, campaigns, editorials, and advocacy messages try to influence opinions, attitudes, or actions.

Remember: A message can inform and persuade at the same time. Always consider its purpose, audience, context, and evidence.
03 / Then and now

Traditional Media

Communication channels that shaped generations.

Traditional media includes long-established forms of mass communication that existed before the widespread use of the internet. These channels helped communities receive shared news, stories, educational content, and entertainment.

📰

Print media

Newspapers, magazines, books, brochures, and pamphlets communicate through printed words and images. Print can provide detailed information that readers can keep and revisit.

Example: A newspaper report about a community event.

📻

Radio

Radio communicates through sound, including spoken reports, interviews, music, and public announcements. It can reach listeners in places where visual media may be less accessible.

Example: A local station broadcasting weather updates.

📺

Television

Television combines moving images, sound, and spoken language to present news, educational programs, documentaries, and entertainment.

Example: A televised news report or science program.

🎞️

Film and cinema

Films use moving images, sound, dialogue, and storytelling to communicate experiences, ideas, culture, and perspectives.

Example: A documentary about environmental protection.

Strengths and limitations: Traditional media can offer edited, structured content and broad reach. However, publication and broadcast schedules may limit how quickly information is updated, and audiences may have fewer opportunities to respond directly.
04 / Connected world

Digital Media

Information created, stored, and shared using digital technology.

Digital media includes content distributed through computers, smartphones, the internet, and other electronic devices. It allows information to move quickly across distances and gives users many ways to create, access, and share content.

🌐

Websites and blogs

Websites publish articles, services, learning resources, portfolios, and updates. Blogs often feature posts written by individuals or groups about particular interests or topics.

📱

Social networking platforms

Social platforms allow users to communicate, join communities, publish posts, exchange ideas, and share photos or videos. Posts can spread quickly, so verification matters.

🎧

Podcasts and streaming

Audio and video streaming services provide interviews, discussions, music, documentaries, and educational programs that audiences can access online.

📸

Digital images and video

Photographs, infographics, animations, and videos can explain complex ideas visually. They should be checked for context, editing, and original source.

Digital responsibility: Protect personal information, respect other people, check privacy settings, credit creators, and pause before reposting claims that may be misleading.
05 / Participation

Interactive Media

Media experiences that respond to the user.

Interactive media allows audiences to do more than receive a message. Users can click, choose, comment, navigate, create, or otherwise influence their experience. Interaction can make learning more engaging, but users should still consider safety, accuracy, and the purpose of the platform.

🎮

Video games

Games invite players to make decisions, solve problems, and interact with digital environments. Some are designed for entertainment, while others support learning or training.

🧭

Interactive websites

Menus, clickable maps, quizzes, forms, and galleries let visitors explore content according to their interests and choices.

🤖

Artificial intelligence

AI tools can help generate, organize, or analyze content. Their outputs still need human review because they may contain errors, omissions, or misleading details.

🥽

Virtual and augmented reality

Virtual reality creates immersive digital environments, while augmented reality adds digital elements to views of the real world.

06 / Think before you share

Media and Information Literacy

Skills for finding, understanding, evaluating, and using information.

Media and Information Literacy (MIL) is the ability to access, analyze, evaluate, create, and communicate information in different forms. It helps people make informed decisions and participate responsibly in society.

A media-literate person does not automatically believe every headline, image, video, or viral post. Instead, they consider the source, look for supporting evidence, recognize possible bias, and compare information with credible references.

Access information

Know how to locate information using libraries, databases, search engines, news outlets, and other resources.

Analyze messages

Identify the creator, intended audience, purpose, language, framing, and context of a message.

Evaluate sources

Check evidence, authorship, publication details, relevance, and whether other trustworthy sources confirm the claims.

Create responsibly

Produce content honestly, respect copyright, represent people fairly, and distinguish facts from opinions.

07 / Source evaluation

The RAVAT Framework

Reliability • Accuracy • Value • Authority • Timeliness

RAVAT is a practical checklist for examining information before using or sharing it. These five considerations help readers judge whether a source is dependable and suitable for a particular purpose. Apply all five rather than relying on only one.

R

Reliability

Reliability asks whether a source can be trusted to provide dependable information. Consider the source's reputation, transparency, consistency, and whether its claims are supported by evidence.

Ask: Does the source have a trustworthy record? Can its claims be checked against other credible sources?

A

Accuracy

Accuracy concerns whether the information is correct, precise, and supported by verifiable facts. Check names, dates, figures, quotations, links, and the evidence behind claims.

Ask: Are the facts correct? Is evidence provided? Do independent sources confirm the details?

V

Value

Value refers to how useful, relevant, and appropriate information is for your topic or purpose. A source may be trustworthy but still not answer the question you are researching.

Ask: Does this information help answer my question? Is it relevant to my audience and purpose?

A

Authority

Authority concerns who created or published the information and what qualifications, experience, or institutional responsibility they have. Look for an author's name, credentials, organization, and contact or editorial information.

Ask: Who is responsible for this content? Do they have relevant knowledge or expertise?

T

Timeliness

Timeliness considers when the information was published, updated, or reviewed and whether it is still current enough for your topic. Some subjects change quickly, while historical facts may remain useful over time.

Ask: When was it published or updated? Does this topic require the latest information?

Important: RAVAT is a guide, not a guarantee. A source can perform well in one area and poorly in another. Always consider the topic and the evidence as a whole.
08 / Put it into practice

Applying RAVAT to an Online Article

Imagine you find an article claiming that a new study has discovered a major health benefit.

Sample situation

A post on a social media page says, “A new study proves that one everyday food prevents all illnesses.” It does not name the study, link to research, identify an author, or show a publication date.

RAVAT criterionWhat should you check?Initial observation
ReliabilityIs the page known for dependable reporting?The post provides no evidence of a reliable track record.
AccuracyCan the broad health claim be verified by research?No study or supporting evidence is linked.
ValueDoes it provide useful information for your purpose?The claim is too broad to guide a sound decision.
AuthorityWho wrote it, and what relevant expertise do they have?No author or qualified source is identified.
TimelinessWhen was it published, and is it current?No publication or update date is shown.

Conclusion: Do not share the claim as fact. Look for the original research, check reputable health institutions, and compare independent sources. The missing information means the post has not established that its claim is trustworthy.

10 / Looking back

Personal Reflection

Learning beyond the screen.

Creating and exploring this portfolio shows that media is more than a tool for entertainment or communication. It influences how people receive information, form opinions, understand issues, and interact with society. Different forms of media offer different opportunities and challenges, so it is important to understand how each one works.

One important lesson is that information should not be accepted simply because it appears online or is shared by many people. The RAVAT framework reminds us to examine Reliability, Accuracy, Value, Authority, and Timeliness. By applying these criteria, we can become more careful readers, responsible creators, and thoughtful members of the digital community.
